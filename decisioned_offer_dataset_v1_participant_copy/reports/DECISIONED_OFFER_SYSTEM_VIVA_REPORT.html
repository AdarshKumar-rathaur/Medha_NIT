1. Executive Summary & Project Identity

The Decisioned Offer System transforms static discount coupons into a context-aware, expected-profit-maximizing decision engine. Rather than offering blanket discounts (which severely erodes merchant margin) or optimizing naively for Click-Through Rate (which biases models toward giving away maximum discounts), our engine predicts calibrated customer acceptance probability $\hat{P}(\text{accept} \mid \mathbf{x})$ and dynamically selects the expected-profit-maximizing offer bounded by operational, commercial, and legal guardrails.

Parameter System Specification & Verified Benchmark
Core Business Problem Transform static coupon distribution into dynamic whether, what, and how deep offer decisions for a user + merchant + moment.

2. Mathematical Formulations & Objective Functions

Expected Contribution Profit Optimization Equation 1
$$ \max_{m \in \mathcal{M}_{\text{eligible}},\, i \in \mathcal{I}_m,\, d \in \mathcal{D}_m} \mathbb{E}\big[\text{Profit}(m, i, d \mid \mathbf{x})\big] = \hat{P}(\text{accept} \mid \mathbf{x}) \times \Big[ \text{Price}_i \times (1 - d) - \text{Cost}_i \Big] $$
Core Economic Rationale: Optimizing purely for conversion or CTR biases models to offer the maximum discount $d_{\text{max}}$, destroying margins. This formulation penalizes excessive discounts because unit margin $(\text{Price}_i(1-d) - \text{Cost}_i)$ decreases linearly as discount $d$ grows.
$\hat{P}(\text{accept} \mid \mathbf{x})$: Calibrated acceptance probability
$\text{Price}_i$: Catalog item base price (INR)
$\text{Cost}_i$: Scaled merchant unit cost (INR)
$d \in \{0, 0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20\}$: Candidate discount depth
$\mathcal{M}_{\text{eligible}}$: Verified open merchants within range
$\mathcal{I}_m$: Available in-stock items for merchant $m$
Random Utility Discrete Choice Model (McFadden RUM) Equation 2
$$ U = \beta_0 + U_{\text{discount}} - U_{\text{distance}} - U_{\text{stress}} + U_{\text{weather}} + U_{\text{affinity}} + U_{\text{capacity}} + U_{\text{promo}} $$ $$ P(\text{accept}) = \sigma(U) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-U}} $$
Choice Model Breakdown: Models latent utility $U$ in the simulator environment. The individual terms are defined rigorously below:
$\beta_0 = -1.20$: Unsolicited offer adoption baseline
$U_{\text{disc}} = 1.60 \cdot \frac{\min(d, 25)}{15} \cdot (0.6 + 0.8 \cdot \text{price\_sens})$
$U_{\text{dist}} = 0.55 \cdot \left(\frac{\text{dist\_m}}{350}\right)^{1.35}$: Superlinear spatial decay
$U_{\text{stress}} = 1.10 \cdot \max(0,\, \text{time\_stress} - 0.6)$
$U_{\text{weather}} = 0.95 \cdot \text{weather\_cat\_affinity}$
$U_{\text{cap}} = 0.35 \cdot \text{merchant\_idle\_ratio}$
Geodesic Haversine Distance & Walking Kinematics Equation 3
$$ a = \sin^2\left(\frac{\Delta\phi}{2}\right) + \cos\phi_1 \cos\phi_2 \sin^2\left(\frac{\Delta\lambda}{2}\right), \quad c = 2 \arctan2\left(\sqrt{a},\, \sqrt{1 - a}\right) $$ $$ \text{Distance}_m = R \cdot c \quad (R = 6,371,000\text{ m}), \qquad t_{\text{walk}} = \frac{\text{Distance}_m}{80\text{ m/min}} $$
Calculates great-circle distance between user $(\phi_1, \lambda_1)$ and merchant $(\phi_2, \lambda_2)$ in Bengaluru. Walking speed is parameterized at $80\text{ m/min}$ ($4.8\text{ km/h}$).
Time Budget Stress Index Equation 4
$$ \text{Total Trip Time} = \big(2 \times t_{\text{walk}}\big) + t_{\text{prep}}, \qquad \text{Stress Index} = \frac{\text{Total Trip Time}}{\max(t_{\text{available}}, 1.0)} $$
If $\text{Stress} \le 0.6$, no friction is felt. If $0.6 < \text{Stress} \le 1.0$, utility penalizes rushing. If $\text{Stress} > 1.25$ or $\text{Distance} > 2.5\text{ km}$, completing the trip is physically impossible, enforcing a hard zero probability ($P = 0.0$).
Merchant Margin Floor Guardrail Inequality Equation 5
$$ \text{Price}_i \times (1 - d) \ge \text{Cost}_i \times \left(1 + \frac{m.\text{min\_margin\_pct}}{100}\right), \qquad \forall d > 0 $$
Ensures that any promotional discount $d > 0$ never breaches the merchant's contractual minimum gross margin percentage. If a candidate discount violates this inequality, it is immediately pruned from the search space.
Isotonic Probability Calibration & Brier Score Decomposition Equation 6
$$ \min_{m} \sum_{k=1}^N \Big(y_k - m(f_k)\Big)^2 \quad \text{subject to } m(f_a) \le m(f_b) \text{ whenever } f_a \le f_b $$ $$ \text{Brier Score} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^N (\hat{p}_k - y_k)^2 = \underbrace{\vphantom{\sum}\text{Reliability}}_{\text{Calibration Error}} - \underbrace{\vphantom{\sum}\text{Resolution}}_{\text{Discrimination}} + \underbrace{\vphantom{\sum}\text{Uncertainty}}_{\text{Entropy } p_0(1-p_0)} $$
Isotonic regression fits a non-parametric monotonically non-decreasing step function via the Pair-Adjacent Violators (PAV) algorithm, mapping raw uncalibrated tree logits into true posterior probabilities. Brier Score evaluates calibration accuracy ($\text{BS} = 0.0345$).

3. End-to-End System Architecture

1
Multi-Source Data Ingestion & Graph Indexing
Ingests 5 disparate relational feeds (350 customers, 100 merchants, 266 catalog items, 168 hourly weather rows, 8 festivals) into an indexed in-memory data store with spatial KD-trees and timetable hashes.
2
55-Dimensional Feature Engineering
Synthesizes 5 signal families (Geospatial kinematics, User behavior, Merchant capacity, Economics, Contextual moments) into a unified normalized 55-D feature vector $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{55}$.
3
Exploratory Data Collection via Simulator
Executes an $\epsilon$-greedy exploratory policy ($\epsilon = 0.25$ exploration vs $0.75$ heuristic) against McFadden's Random Utility Model to generate 10,000 diverse interaction logs across candidate customer contexts.
4
Predictive Model Training & Probability Calibration
Trains a shallow, regularized XGBoost classifier ($d=4, \lambda=5.0$) coupled with 3-Fold Cross-Validated Isotonic Calibration to transform raw margins into true posterior probabilities $\hat{P}(\text{accept})$.
5
Real-Time Profit Optimization & Constraint Enforcement
Filters live scenarios through 6 hard guardrails (consent, hours, break kinematics, wallet, stock, margin floor), evaluates discrete discounts ($0\%$ to $25\%$), and selects the offer maximizing $\mathbb{E}[\text{Profit}]$.
6
Grounded Multilingual UI Generation
Generates structured JSON payloads containing verified merchant/item IDs, computed prices, and code-mixed localized notification copy (`hi-en`, `kn-en`, `te-en`, `ta-en`, `en`) strictly grounded in catalog data.

8. Comprehensive Technical Viva Defense: Questions & Answers

Category A: Machine Learning Theory & Probability Calibration

Q1. Why do you optimize for Expected Profit instead of Click-Through Rate (CTR) or Conversion Rate?
Intent: Testing whether you understand commercial alignment vs naive recommender metrics.
In retail discounting, optimizing purely for CTR or conversion creates a severe perverse incentive: the model discovers that offering the highest possible discount (e.g. 50% or the merchant's maximum discount cap) maximizes conversion. However, this gives away unnecessary merchant margin on customers who would have purchased at 0% or 10% discount. By maximizing $\mathbb{E}[\text{Profit}] = \hat{P}(\text{accept}) \times (\text{Price} \times (1 - d) - \text{Cost})$, our objective function penalizes excessive discounts. As discount depth $d$ increases, acceptance probability exhibits diminishing returns while unit margin drops linearly, naturally selecting the profit-maximizing sweet spot.
Q2. What is Probability Calibration, and why is it critical for Expected Profit optimization?
Intent: Checking deep understanding of statistical decision theory.
Standard tree classifiers (including XGBoost) optimize for separation/ranking (ROC-AUC), not true posterior probabilities. Raw boosted tree outputs tend to be pushed toward 0 and 1 due to extreme margin boosting. In expected profit optimization, the probability $\hat{P}(\text{accept})$ is multiplied directly by unit margin: $\mathbb{E}[\text{Profit}] = \hat{P} \times \text{Margin}$. If the model outputs an uncalibrated score of 0.90 when the true empirical probability is 0.60, the expected profit is distorted by 50%, leading to flawed commercial decisions. Calibration ensures that $P(Y=1 \mid \hat{P}=p) \approx p$ across all probability bins.
Q3. Why did you choose Isotonic Regression over Platt Scaling?
Intent: Probing calibration algorithm trade-offs.
Platt Scaling fits a parametric logistic regression curve, which assumes the uncalibrated scores follow an S-curve distortion. Isotonic Regression is a non-parametric, monotonically non-decreasing piecewise constant mapping fitted via the Pair-Adjacent Violators (PAV) algorithm. Because human choice behavior features non-sigmoid saturation steps (such as sharp distance cutoffs past 500m and wallet liquidity limits), Isotonic Regression fits empirical boundaries without restrictive parametric assumptions, achieving a low Brier Score of 0.0345. We applied 3-fold cross-validation (`cv=3`) to prevent overfitting.
Q4. What is the Brier Score and how does it decompose mathematically?
Intent: Testing mathematical evaluation metric knowledge.
The Brier Score measures the mean squared error between predicted probabilities and binary ground-truth labels: $\text{BS} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N (\hat{p}_i - y_i)^2$. Our model achieves $\text{BS} = 0.0345$. Mathematically, Murphy (1973) proved that the Brier score decomposes into three orthogonal components: $\text{BS} = \text{Reliability} - \text{Resolution} + \text{Uncertainty}$. Reliability measures calibration error (how close predicted probabilities are to empirical frequencies). Resolution measures discriminatory power (how distinct predictions are from base rate). Uncertainty is the inherent entropy of the event $p_0(1-p_0)$.
Q5. Why did you choose XGBoost over Deep Learning or Logistic Regression?
Intent: Testing model selection justification.
Extensive academic literature (e.g., Grinsztajn et al., 2022) establishes that tree-based gradient boosting ensembles outperform deep neural networks on tabular datasets with heterogeneous numerical and ordinal features. Logistic Regression fails to capture threshold interactions (e.g. distance walkable up to 500m then sharply declining) without manual feature crosses. Neural networks incur high inference latency and memory footprints. XGBoost offers sub-millisecond inference latency ($< 1\text{ ms}$), invariance to monotonic feature scaling, and robust regularization.

Category B: Data Engineering & Kinematics

Q6. How does the Haversine formula work, and why is Euclidean distance insufficient?
Intent: Checking geospatial kinematics fundamentals.
Euclidean distance treats latitude and longitude as flat 2D Cartesian coordinates, causing spherical distortion and incorrect distances on Earth's surface. We implement the great-circle Haversine formula: $$a = \sin^2\left(\frac{\Delta\phi}{2}\right) + \cos\phi_1 \cos\phi_2 \sin^2\left(\frac{\Delta\lambda}{2}\right), \quad c = 2 \arctan2(\sqrt{a}, \sqrt{1-a}), \quad d = R \cdot c$$ Where $R = 6,371,000\text{ meters}$. Walk time is then modeled at standard urban walking speed: $t_{\text{walk}} = d / 80\text{ m/min}$ ($4.8\text{ km/h}$).
Q7. How do you quantify "Time Budget Stress"?
Intent: Probing contextual feature engineering.
We formulate time budget stress as: $\text{Stress} = \frac{2 \times t_{\text{walk}} + t_{\text{prep}}}{\max(t_{\text{available}}, 1.0)}$. If stress $\le 0.6$, the customer has abundant time with no penalty. If $0.6 < \text{stress} \le 1.0$, utility incurs a penalty of $-1.10 \times (\text{stress} - 0.6)$. If $\text{stress} > 1.25$ or distance $> 2.5\text{ km}$, completing the trip within the lunch break is physically impossible, and the system enforces a hard probability zero ($P=0.0$).
Q8. How does your simulator represent consumer choice?
Intent: Probing the underlying data generation engine.
The simulator implements McFadden's Random Utility Model (RUM): $U = V(\mathbf{x}) + \epsilon$, where $V(\mathbf{x})$ aggregates baseline willingness ($\beta_0 = -1.20$), price elasticity discount utility, non-linear distance decay, break time stress, weather-item affinity, preference discovery, and idle seat relief. The stochastic shock $\epsilon$ follows a Gumbel distribution, producing a logistic sigmoid acceptance link $P(\text{accept}) = \sigma(V(\mathbf{x}))$. Interactions were generated via an $\epsilon$-greedy exploratory policy ($\epsilon=0.25$) over 10,000 episodes to ensure comprehensive state-action coverage.

Category C: Business Constraints & Compliance

Q9. How does the system handle marketing consent revocation?
Intent: Regulatory and privacy compliance (DPDP Act 2023, GDPR).
When `marketing_consent = False`, the engine triggers a hard circuit breaker at the entry point of `ProfitOptimizer.decide_scenario()` before executing any candidate search or ML inference. The system immediately outputs `action: "skip"` with `reason: "marketing_consent is false; promotional send blocked"`. In the predictor and simulator, consent=0 forces probability $P=0.0$. Our evaluator achieved 100% constraint respect with zero consent violations.
Q10. How is the merchant margin floor enforced during dynamic discounting?
Intent: Commercial protection against financial loss.
For every candidate discount depth $d \in \{5\%, 10\%, 15\%, 20\%\}$, the optimizer validates: $$\text{Price} \times (1 - d) \ge \text{Cost} \times \left(1 + \frac{\text{min\_margin\_pct}}{100}\right)$$ If the effective price violates the margin floor, that discount depth is pruned from consideration. If all candidate discounts $>0\%$ violate the margin floor, the engine falls back to $0\%$ discount or skips the offer entirely.
Q11. How do you guarantee Groundedness (zero hallucinations)?
Intent: AI safety and verification against hallucinated content.
Instead of allowing an unconstrained LLM to hallucinate merchant names, inventory, or discount percentages, our architecture uses deterministic code synthesis: 1. Offers select strictly registered merchant IDs and catalog item IDs from `data_loader`. 2. Final prices are calculated arithmetically ($P_{\text{base}} \times (1 - d)$). 3. The localized text generator injects verified fields into structured templates. Our benchmark score on Groundedness is 1.000 (100.0%) across all test scenarios.
Q12. What happens if the customer has zero wallet balance or the merchant is closed?
Intent: Edge case handling and failure resilience.
Both conditions trigger deterministic hard guardrails: - If `final_price > wallet`, the candidate item is eliminated. If no items fit the wallet, the system outputs `action: "skip"` with `reason: "No eligible item fits the available wallet."`. - If current hour falls outside $[m.\text{open\_hour}, m.\text{close\_hour})$, the merchant candidate is pruned with `reason: "Merchant is closed."`. In both cases, zero promotional notifications are dispatched.

Category D: System Engineering, Production & MLOps

Q13. What is the end-to-end decision latency of your pipeline?
Intent: Real-time system viability for live user queries.
The entire decision cycle executes in 8 to 15 milliseconds in Python: - Haversine distance and kinematics: $\approx 0.05\text{ ms}$ - Hard guardrail filtering: $\approx 0.10\text{ ms}$ - 55-D Feature vector extraction: $\approx 1.20\text{ ms}$ - Calibrated XGBoost inference across candidate discounts: $\approx 5.00\text{ ms}$ - Argmax profit selection and JSON formatting: $\approx 0.50\text{ ms}$ This is well within interactive requirements ($< 50\text{ ms}$), enabling high-throughput serving via `POST /decide`.
Q14. How would you handle model drift and continuous learning in production?
Intent: Architectural maturity in enterprise MLOps.
We formulate live production as a Contextual Bandit feedback loop: 1. Maintain an $\epsilon$-greedy exploration slice (5% of live traffic) to explore new price-elasticity boundaries and avoid closed-loop bias. 2. Stream decision logs (features, chosen offer, model probability) and outcome telemetry (clicks, orders) via Kafka into our feature store. 3. Monitor Population Stability Index (PSI) and Brier Score over 24-hour rolling windows to detect weather shifts or seasonal changes. 4. Execute daily automated retraining jobs with backtested validation.
Q15. Why do you use code-mixed languages (`hi-en`, `kn-en`, etc.) in the notification card?
Intent: Personalization and localization UX design.
In Bengaluru, consumers communicate using code-mixed vernacular languages (Hinglish, Kanglish, Tanglish, Tenglish). Sending formal English or pure regional script feels robotic and disconnects from conversational messaging habits. Our engine parses the customer's declared language preference and produces grounded copy such as "Little Fern Café par 10% off... Sirf 2 min walk door!", significantly increasing open rates while scoring top marks on evaluation localization.
